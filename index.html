How this works

Five short sessions a week, drawn from a rolling seven-session cycle. Every session trains the whole body through the six patterns daily life actually asks for: hinge, squat, push, pull, carry, brace.

Seven sessions per cycle, each pattern twice, legs never shortchanged. At five sessions a week the cycle rolls through in about eight days — which is the point. The rotation follows what you did, not the calendar. Miss Tuesday and Wednesday simply becomes the session you missed.

The 4-week wave

Week 1 — Groovestop 3 reps short
Week 2 — Pressstop 2 reps short
Week 3 — Peak+1 set, 1 rep short
Week 4 — Deload−1 set, easy

Then the wave restarts with harder variations on the exercises you levelled up. Deload weeks are not optional. They are where the strength you built actually shows up.

Progressing with bands

You cannot add 2.5 kg to a band, so you progress in this order:

  • Reps — reach the top of the range on every set
  • Tension — shorten your grip or stand wider on the band
  • Tempo — 3 seconds lowering, no bounce at the bottom
  • Leverage — one limb instead of two, or a longer range
  • Band — only then move to the next colour up

When you hit the top of the range on all sets and it still felt easy, tap Level up on that exercise. The app remembers per exercise.

Bands you want

A set of three or four loop bands (light / medium / heavy) plus a door anchor covers everything in this plan. If you buy one more thing later, make it a door-frame pull-up bar — vertical pulling is the one gap bands never fully close.

How to spread it

Protein is the only macro where timing earns you anything, and even then it is modest. What matters is hitting — g in each of four feedings rather than 20 g at lunch and the rest at 21:00.

WhenWhatProtein

Training-day timing

  • Before an early shift: 20 minutes of band work needs no pre-fuel. Coffee, train, then a real breakfast with — g protein inside the hour.
  • After the shift: eat something at 15:00–16:00 so you are not training on an empty tank at 19:00. A banana and skyr is enough.
  • Late finish, no appetite: a shake counts. A missed feeding you never make up is worse than a shake you did not feel like.
  • Rest day: same protein, same food. Muscle is repaired on the days you are not training.

OR-day survival

The real threat to this plan is not the training — it is a 10-hour list where you eat nothing and then inhale 1,400 kcal at 22:00. Two defences:

  • The bag never leaves. Two protein bars or a shaker with powder, permanently in your work bag, restocked Sunday. Not a plan, a reflex.
  • Front-load. On a known long list, take — g protein at breakfast instead of your usual. You cannot bank calories, but you can stop bleeding protein.

Sleep

With your schedule this is the variable with the most leverage and the least control. Two things that survive shift work: keep the wake time steadier than the bedtime, and after a night shift sleep in one block in the morning rather than napping across the day. On weeks where sleep collapses, train the Reset day and drop a session — do not push a Peak week on four hours' sleep.
